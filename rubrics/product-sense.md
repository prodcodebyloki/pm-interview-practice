# Product Sense Rubric

Derived from `frameworks/product-sense.md`. It has seven dimensions: the five framework steps, with Step 4 split into three parts (brainstorming, selection and v1, metrics and risks).

## Scoring rules

- **Scale 1-5.** 1 = absent or wrong. 2 = attempted but weak. 3 = competent, a solid pass. 4 = strong. 5 = exceptional and rare; reserve it for answers you would quote to a hiring committee.
- **Be strict.** When torn between two scores, give the lower one.
- **Every score needs evidence.** Give a short verbatim quote from the candidate (≤ 25 words). If there is nothing to quote, the score is 1 and the evidence is "not attempted".
- **Skipped stage:** score 1 with evidence "skipped". If the candidate *chose* to compress the stage and said so (for example, "I'll keep motivation brief given time"), score what was said, with no automatic 1.
- **Hints:** a dimension where the candidate used a hint is capped at 3.
- **Adaptation over recitation.** Using framework words without applying them to the case ("now I'll do a 2x2") earns no credit. Credit comes from case-specific content. Reciting the template while ignoring the case is capped at 2.
- **Overall score** = mean of the 7 dimensions, to one decimal. **Verdict: Pass** only if every dimension is ≥ 3. Brilliance in one dimension cannot rescue weakness in another.

| # | Dimension | Framework step |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Clear communication | Step 0 + throughout |
| D2 | Product motivation | Step 1 |
| D3 | Segmentation | Step 2 |
| D4 | Problem identification | Step 3 |
| D5 | Solution brainstorming | Step 4, part 1 |
| D6 | Solution selection & v1 | Step 4, parts 2-3 |
| D7 | Success metrics & risks | Step 4, part 3 |

## D1. Clear communication

Covers the opening (restate, assumptions, game plan) and conduct throughout: signposting, check-ins, adapting to redirection, and a closing that ties back to the mission.

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Dives in with no restatement, assumptions or plan. Stream of consciousness. Asks "what should I do next?" |
| 3 | Restates the question, sets 2-4 relevant assumptions, states a plan. Some signposting. ≥ 2 check-ins. Gives a closing summary. |
| 5 | Crisp opening whose assumptions are chosen *for this case*. Separates process from conclusion every time ("let me list options… I'm choosing B because…"). ≥ 3 well-timed check-ins. Adapts smoothly to redirection. The closing ties back to the mission. |

**Evidence to quote:** the restatement, the assumptions, the game plan, a waypoint line, the closing.
**Failure modes:** generic assumptions that would fit any question. Assumptions that close off solutions (premature narrowing). No check-ins. Asking the interviewer to drive. Rambling with no "so my conclusion is…". No closing.

## D2. Product motivation

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Lists features only. No mission, or a slogan like "help users be more productive". |
| 3 | Names the human need behind the product, gives some strategic context and 1-2 competitors, and states a usable mission. Takes about 3-5 min. |
| 5 | Moves from feature to a deep human need, ties it to company strategy and strengths, and names what is unique vs. 2-3 competitors. The mission is specific yet open (fits the formula) and visibly guides later choices. |

**Evidence to quote:** the human-need line, the competitor and strategy line, the mission statement.
**Failure modes:** features without purpose. A mission too vague or too narrow. A mission never used again. Ignoring company strengths. Serving the company but not users. Spending more than 6 min.

## D3. Segmentation

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Jumps to one demographic group ("millennials"). No ecosystem view, no prioritization. |
| 3 | Lists ecosystem players and picks one with a reason. Gives three motivation- or behavior-based segments. Prioritizes with reach vs. underserved. Gives a persona. |
| 5 | Four or five ecosystem players with a mission-tied choice and a check-in. Three mutually exclusive segments, with the exclusivity tested out loud. Decisive High/Low placement plus a strategic reason (mission fit, early adopters, learning). A vivid persona with constraints and a goal. Brisk on dimensions. |

**Evidence to quote:** the ecosystem choice and its rationale, the three segments, the prioritization line, the persona.
**Failure modes:** demographic-only segments. Overlapping segments. Hedging ("medium-ish"). More or fewer than three segments without reason. Skipping the ecosystem. Listing too many segmentation properties and burning the clock. A generic persona.

## D4. Problem identification

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | States needs or solutions as problems ("users need a better algorithm"). No journey. |
| 3 | A product-specific journey. Three real obstacles. Scored on severity and frequency. One chosen. |
| 5 | A 5-7 stage journey specific to the persona and product. Three problems in "[persona] struggles to [task] because [cause], which leads to [consequence]" form, with root causes. Scoring said out loud with justification. The choice is explicitly tied to the mission. |

**Evidence to quote:** a journey stage, each problem statement, the prioritization line.
**Failure modes:** need/problem confusion. Problem-solution conflation. A generic before/during/after journey. Symptoms instead of root causes. Picking a problem with no scoring. No link to the mission or persona.

## D5. Solution brainstorming

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | One idea only (solution jumping), or a feature list unrelated to the problem. |
| 3 | Three ideas that each address the chosen problem, with some difference in approach. |
| 5 | Three truly distinct angles (for example automation vs. guidance vs. community), each explicitly linked to the problem and to a company asset. At least one non-obvious idea, such as a cross-domain analogy or thoughtful use of new technology. |

**Evidence to quote:** each of the three ideas as stated.
**Failure modes:** three variations of one idea. Feature obsession. Ideas that solve a different problem. Ignoring the company's unique assets (platform mismatch).

## D6. Solution selection & v1

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Picks with no criteria, or never picks. No v1. |
| 3 | Uses impact vs. effort to pick one with reasoning. A v1 that says what it does and who gets it. |
| 5 | Decisive quadrant placement and a mention of defensibility. A smallest-possible v1: who, what, and what is explicitly out. Discovery and distribution in existing surfaces. A one-line vision beyond v1. The persona's "after" state is clear. |

**Evidence to quote:** the prioritization line, the v1 scope, the out-of-scope items, the distribution line.
**Failure modes:** a v1 that is the full vision. No out-of-scope list. No distribution plan. Picking high-effort ideas with no justification. No defensibility.

## D7. Success metrics & risks

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | No metrics, or vanity metrics only ("downloads"). No risks. |
| 3 | One primary metric tied to the problem, at least one guardrail, and two risks with mitigations. |
| 5 | A primary metric that measures the problem being solved (with a time window). Guardrails that catch the solution's likely harms. Two or three specific risks (including safety or privacy where relevant) with concrete mitigations. A "what I'd do next" experiment. |

**Evidence to quote:** the primary metric, the guardrail(s), each risk and its mitigation.
**Failure modes:** vanity or activity metrics. A primary metric not linked to the chosen problem. No guardrail. Generic risks ("it might not work"). Risks with no mitigation. Running out of time before reaching this stage.
